2.1 · Finding 1

Algorithmic source selection rarely produced results.

67 real queries · 20+ public endpoints · one year

Key messageAlgorithmic federation was far less reliable than manual federation.

All runs, Spring 2025 – Spring 2026: manual 47.3% non-empty / 46.2% explicit errors · algorithmic 3.0% / 95.1%; rounded to whole squares. Source: Crum et al., ISWC 2026 · every query and run: results explorer · study set-up: S2.

2.2 · Why it fails

Probing every source exceeds what endpoints can serve.

Key messageEngines that plan blind cannot adapt to limits they cannot see.

1 One algorithmic run of query 6, before it failed. 2 Runs with and without client-side rate-limit mitigation had similar success rates (Crum et al., ISWC 2026, Sec. 5.4).

2.3 · Finding 2

Over one year, both approaches answered fewer queries.

Key messagePublic endpoints evolve as a matter of course; federated queries must be robust to it.

Small dots = individual runs (25); large dots = period average. Manual runs: Spring 2025 and Spring 2026; algorithmic runs from Fall 2025 on. 1 As reported by UniProt's server administrator; 68.7% of queries varied in result count (Crum et al., ISWC 2026, Sec. 5).

2.4 · Moving forward

VoRD: endpoints describe their limits, machine-readably.

Key messageIf endpoints publish their limits, engines and users can stay within them.

1 VoRD, Vocabulary of Restrictive Datasets (v0.2); extends SPARQL 1.1 Service Description; validated with SHACL and ShEx; its documentation site is built with the Ontology Companion Generator (part 3). Card values: VoRD's Wikidata example, derived from Wikimedia's public rate-limit documentation (May 2026).

2.5 · Next steps

Next: query engines that read the limits and adapt.

Key messageEngines that read published limits can adapt instead of probing blindly.

Directions from Crum et al., ISWC 2026 (Sec. 6): request budgets, endpoint-aware planning, adaptive back-off, diagnostics and safeguards against request explosion. HTTP 429 Too Many Requests: RFC 6585 · the same loop applies to querying many personal pods (part 1).

Built on rdf-parse, Graphology and Sigma.js; pitfalls from OOPS!; set up with npx ocg init --ontology vocab/my-vocabulary.ttl; step-by-step in the Usage Guide. 1 Mechanism as described in the OCG README; hash IRIs return the whole ontology, not one term.

4.1 · Why

VCF keeps meaning in its structure, not in its values.

Key messageBoth readings look plausible; lose the structure and evidence lands on the wrong allele.

1 Number=LR in the VCF 4.5 specification; example from Listing 1 of the VCF Core manuscript (SWAT4HCLS 2027). 2 As framed in the VCF-RDFizer manuscript (BioMedSem, in preparation).

4.2 · How: the vocabulary

VCF Core turns interpretation rules into relationships.

Key messageforAllele resolves the local-allele rule once, at conversion; every query reuses it.

VCF Core v2.1.3: version-specific for VCF 4.1–4.5, constraints as SHACL shapes, 123 SSSOM alignments to other models; model from SWAT4HCLS 2027, Fig. 1. 2 10,000 records with 2,504 samples (VCF-RDFizer manuscript); condensed keeps every value, inside literals.

4.3 · How: the converter

VCF-RDFizer: convert, check, then link and govern.

Key messageEach conversion is checked against its source; links and policies stay separate data.

1 RML (RMLStreamer) emits the nine-triple record backbone, about 5% of the graph; direct emitters supply the rest. VCF-RDFizer on PyPI, Conda-Forge and Docker (MIT); design from the VCF-RDFizer manuscript; SPDI: Holmes et al., Bioinformatics.

4.4 · What it means: faithful

The graph keeps what the file says, and we can check it.

Key messageTests derived from the specification catch errors that plausible-looking output would hide.

1 VCF Core manuscript, RQ1–RQ2: every case also passes empty-graph and predicate-deletion controls; most missing coverage is untested, not failing. 2 VCF-RDFizer manuscript: fixtures and slices up to 17.1M triples; the deep shape profiles are too costly for routine large-file use.

4.5 · What it means: linked

Same answers as a bcftools pipeline, plus reusable links.

Key messageRDF is not needed for the answer; it makes the links and release rules reusable data.

1 ACMG SF v3.2 gene list; ClinVar release 2026-09-13, at least one review star; classifications retrieved, not clinically adjudicated. 2 Participant–variant–gene matches, not people; all consents simulated; also run on five genomes and on whole-genome HG005 (VCF-RDFizer manuscript).

4.6 · What it means: costs

It pays off when questions are repeated, not one-off.

Key messageUse RDF for repeated, linked or governed questions; native tools for one-pass analyses.

1 One scenario, not a constant: 423.7 s conversion + 22.8 s indexing, ≈ 10.2 s saved per question vs. ≈ 12 s parser scans. 2 17.1M-triple graph; 11,160 executions, no answer disagreements; QLever. Source: VCF-RDFizer manuscript.

S4 · VCF-RDFizer

The VCF-RDFizer pipeline.

Convert
VCF to RDF (N-Triples) with RMLStreamer, targeting the VCF Core vocabulary (vcfc:)
Compress
Optional compression into queryable HDT and COTTAS artifacts
Validate
Semantic validation of the compressed graph against its source VCF
Link
A provenance-tracked side-graph of links to external resources
Protect
ODRL policies attached to files, regions and variants, with checked release views

Available via PyPI, conda-forge and Docker. Source: github.com/ecrum19/VCF-RDFizer ↗ · Vocabulary: w3id.org/vcf-core/vocab ↗
